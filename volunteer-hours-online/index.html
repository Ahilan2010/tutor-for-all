01 What schools look for

What makes volunteer hours count

  • A real organization that can confirm your work.
  • A record of dates and hours.
  • Someone who can sign for it, usually in a letter or on your school’s form.
  • Work that helps people, not just yourself.

02 Programs

Where tutoring hours often count

  • National Honor Society. Each chapter sets its own service rules, so ask your adviser.
  • High school graduation requirements. Many schools and districts require service hours.
  • IB CAS. Tutoring can be a strong Service experience. Talk to your CAS coordinator first.
  • Ontario’s 40 community involvement hours. Your school must approve the activity, so check before you start.
  • Scholarships and college applications. Teaching one child for months shows real commitment.
  • The President’s Volunteer Service Award was paused in 2025. Check its website for the latest.

03 How it works here

How hours work at Tutor-For-All

  • One verified hour for each session. You log it after each session and we verify it.
  • Referrals count too: 2 hours for each tutor you refer and 30 minutes for each family, once they meet the rules.
  • Proof when you need it. Download a certificate from your private hours page, or ask for a signed letter or a school form.
  • Check first. Every school decides what it accepts, so ask yours before you start.

04 Who can apply

Students anywhere can tutor

You need to be in high school or college, with strong grades and an hour or two a week. Tutoring happens over Google Meet, so you can do it from any country. You do not need to speak English: we match you with children who speak your language.
