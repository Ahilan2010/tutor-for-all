01 About Tutor-For-All

What is Tutor-For-All?

A student-led nonprofit that gives children around the world free, one-on-one, online math and reading tutoring for kindergarten through 5th-grade students from low-income families. Our tutors are high school and college student volunteers.

Who started it?

Students at Carnegie Vanguard High School in Houston, Texas. We saw elementary kids falling behind because their families couldn't afford private tutoring, which often costs $40 to $80 an hour, and decided to do something about it.

Is it really free?

Yes. Families never pay anything: no sign-up fee, no session fees, no materials to buy, now or later. Tutors are volunteers, and our small costs (books, learning tools, outreach) are covered by donations.

Where do you operate?

Worldwide. Every session happens online, so we match tutors and families across countries and time zones. Both sign-up forms ask for your country and time zone. We were founded by students in Houston, Texas.

Which languages do you work in?

This website is available in several languages: use the language menu at the top of the page. Tutoring happens in any language we have a tutor for, including English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Arabic, Hindi, Vietnamese, Mandarin and many others. Our translations are made by computer, so if anything is unclear, the English page is the official one.

How can I contact you?

Email tutorforallnonprofit@gmail.com or message us on Instagram @tutorforallorg. A real person on our student team will reply.

02 For parents & guardians

Who can sign up?

Students in kindergarten through 5th grade from low-income households. The program is reserved for families who can't access paid tutoring.

Can you work with children who have learning disabilities or special needs?

Unfortunately, no. Our tutors are high school and college student volunteers, not trained teachers or special education specialists, so we can't accommodate learning disabilities, developmental or behavioral conditions, or other needs that call for specialized instruction. We'd encourage you to ask your child's school about support services.

How do you decide if we qualify?

The sign-up form asks a few short, confidential questions. In the United States: whether your child gets free or reduced-price school lunch, your approximate household income range, and whether your household receives assistance such as SNAP, Medicaid/CHIP, WIC, TANF or housing assistance. In other countries: whether your child gets free or low-cost school meals or help with school fees, how your income compares with most families in your country, and whether you receive government or charity help. We use the answers only to make sure our limited spots go to the families who need them most.

I'm not sure we qualify. Should I still apply?

Yes. Fill out the form, or email tutorforallnonprofit@gmail.com first if you'd rather ask. We'd rather hear from you than have you miss out.

Which subjects do you teach?

Math and reading/ELA, or both. In math that runs from counting and shapes in kindergarten to fractions, decimals and early algebraic thinking in 5th grade. In reading it runs from phonics and letter sounds to comprehension and vocabulary. See the full grade-by-grade list.

How do I sign up?

Fill out the student sign-up form. It takes about five minutes and asks about your child, your contact details, the times that work for you, and a few eligibility questions.

How long until we hear back?

We review every request and reach out within 3–5 school days, by email or phone, whichever you prefer. We may ask a short follow-up question.

How do you choose my child's tutor?

We match by grade level, subjects, language, and the times you are both available. You can also ask for a male or female tutor. A less common language or a gender choice can mean a longer wait.

My child is learning English. Can you help?

Yes. The form asks which language the tutor should speak with your child, and whether your child is learning English. We match them with a tutor who speaks that language, which can take longer. If English is fine too, tick that box for a shorter wait.

How long will my child get tutoring?

As long as they need it. Some families want help for a few weeks, others for a semester or a full school year. The form asks for your best estimate, and you're not locked in.

We don't speak English. Can we still sign up?

Yes. Fill in the form in your language and tell us which language your child speaks best. We match your child with a tutor who speaks it. Our emails reach you in your language, with the English underneath. You can also reply to us in your own language.

Do I need to pay for books or materials?

No. Nothing to buy. We also give away donated children's books to the students we serve.

A tutor referred me. Does that matter?

There's an optional question on the form for the name of the Tutor-For-All tutor who referred you. It helps us credit them for spreading the word.

03 Sessions, safety & technology

How do sessions work?

One tutor and one student, online over Google Meet, for about an hour a week. Tutors plan around your child's grade level and the specific skills they need help with, including anything you tell us they're struggling with on the sign-up form.

Are sessions ever in person?

No. Every session is online, so no one comes to your home and you don't need to drive anywhere.

Who are the tutors?

High school and college students with strong grades: at least a 3.5 unweighted GPA and a 90 or above in the subject they teach. Every tutor is reviewed by our team before being accepted.

How do you keep my child safe?

Before a tutor is matched with anyone, they complete our safety training and agree to our Code of Conduct. Our team also meets each new tutor on a short video call, or reviews a short video from them. Sessions are online only, and each call is closed to everyone except the people invited. A parent or guardian can sit in on any session. Read how we keep children safe.

Can I watch the sessions?

Please do. A parent or guardian is welcome, and encouraged, to sit in on any session from the same room.

Do we need a Google account?

Not to sign up. Sessions run on Google Meet, and a free Google account is the easiest way in: sign in with the account on your invitation and you go straight to the call. Without one, someone has to let you in, so you may have to wait. If you can, give us a Google email on the form.

What equipment does my child need?

An internet connection and a device with a camera and microphone, ideally a laptop or tablet. If you only have a phone, or your connection is unreliable, say so on the form. We still want to hear from you.

How does scheduling work across time zones?

On the form, pick the hours that work in your own local time. Don't convert anything; we handle it. Checking two hours in a row (for example 4–5pm and 5–6pm) helps a lot, because clocks change on different dates in different countries and a session can shift by an hour.

What if we need to miss or move a session?

Tap “Cancel this session” in the reminder email we send the day before, at least 2 hours before the session starts. Your tutor is told right away, and it does not count against you. To move a session or take a break for a few weeks, reply to any of our emails. How cancelling works.

What happens to our information?

We use it only to check eligibility, match your child with a tutor, and contact you about scheduling. We never sell it or share it with anyone outside Tutor-For-All. Read our privacy notice.

04 For tutors

Who can become a tutor?

High school students (grades 9–12, or the same age in your country) and college or university students. You need at least a 3.5 unweighted GPA or the same level in your country's grading system, a 90 or above in the subject you want to tutor, reliable internet and a device, and one to two hours a week.

Can college students apply?

Yes. College and university students are welcome on the same terms as high schoolers. Choose “College / University” as your year on the application.

I live outside the U.S. Can I tutor?

Yes. Sessions are online, so you can tutor from any country and time zone. The application asks for your country and time zone, and you list your availability in your own local time. If your school does not use GPA or percent grades, tell us your grades your way on the form.

How exactly do the grade requirements work?

To tutor math, you need a 90 or above in your current or most recent math course. To tutor reading, the same in your language class. To tutor both, we look at both. On top of that, you need at least a 3.5 GPA on a 4.0 unweighted scale, or the same level in your country's grading system.

I'm just below the requirements. Should I apply?

Yes. If you're close, apply anyway. We review every application individually. If it's not a yes this time, you're welcome to apply again next semester.

Will you check my grades?

Yes, we may verify your academic qualifications. The application includes an electronic signature confirming your grades are accurate, and misrepresenting them means removal from the program. You also agree to keep meeting the minimum standards while you tutor.

What happens after I apply?

We review your application and reach out within 3–5 school days. If accepted, you get a link to our onboarding: short training videos, five questions on how sessions work and how we keep students safe, our Code of Conduct, and your availability. Prefer another language? You can read the whole training and take the quiz in your language. Then you do a short hello video call, or send us a short video of yourself, and we match you with a student.

How much time does it take?

At least one to two hours a week, scheduled around your availability. Sessions are about an hour each. Consistency matters more than volume: your student is counting on you.

Do I need tutoring experience?

No. Strong grades, patience and reliability matter most. Onboarding covers how sessions work. If you do have experience, mention it on the application.

Do I need to speak English to tutor?

No. If your English is limited, we match you only with children who speak your language. This website, your emails and your training can all be in your language.

Can I tutor in a language other than English?

Yes. You do not need to speak English to tutor with us. Tell us your languages on the application. We match you only with children who speak your language, and your emails and training come in that language too.

Can I get a reference letter?

Yes. Tutors can request a reference letter from our team for internships, jobs, scholarships, or college applications.

05 Volunteer hours

How many hours do I earn?

One verified hour per tutoring session, two hours for each tutor you refer, and thirty minutes for each family you refer, once the referral is verified.

What can I use my hours for?

Hours are verified by the Tutor-For-All team and can be used for NHS requirements, graduation requirements, IB CAS or college service-learning, scholarship applications, and college or job applications. Check your school's or program's own rules for what they accept. Read our guide to online volunteer hours.

How do I log my hours?

Submit the volunteer hour log within 3 days of every session, and after any other qualifying activity: one submission per session. If a session has no log, we'll email you to ask what happened.

Why weren't my hours verified?

The most common reason is inconsistent entries. Type your full name and your school's name exactly the same way every time, with no nicknames or abbreviations. If something still looks wrong, email tutorforallnonprofit@gmail.com.

When do referral hours count?

For a tutor referral (2 hours), the person you referred must name you on their application, be accepted, finish onboarding, and tutor at least one verified session. For a family referral (30 minutes), the family must name you on their sign-up form, and the student must complete their first session.

06 Donating

How can I donate?

Give money securely through PayPal (you can pay by card without a PayPal account), or donate books and school supplies through our donation form, and we'll arrange drop-off or pickup.

Where does the money go?

We have no paid staff. Donations go roughly 65% to educational materials and books, 20% to technology and learning tools, and 15% to outreach and program operations.

What books and supplies do you accept?

Children's books, workbooks, pencils, notebooks and other school supplies. See the full list of accepted donations.

Can my organization partner with you?

We'd love that. Bookstores and book nonprofits have already helped us. Email tutorforallnonprofit@gmail.com to talk.
